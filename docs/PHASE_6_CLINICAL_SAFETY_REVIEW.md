# TriageX Phase 6 — Clinical Safety Review

**Review date:** 2026-11-10
**Status:** Demonstration-only source review. No clinical validation or certification.

## Summary

The current triage engine is deterministic JavaScript heuristic logic, not an AI model and not a clinically validated triage protocol. It uses keyword matching, hardcoded thresholds, simple text parsing, and generated suggestions. It must not be used to diagnose, rule out emergencies, recommend treatment, determine a safe wait time, or direct real patient care.

The application already includes warnings that it is a decision-support demonstration and instructs users to use synthetic data only. This audit strengthens those warnings by removing the generated confidence percentage and correcting the claim that image metadata had been visually analyzed. The underlying clinical rules remain unvalidated and require qualified clinical review before any care use.

## Observed behavior and risks

- Symptom and vital rules produce an urgency category and red-flag output; thresholds and mappings have not been independently established or clinically validated.
- The engine can suggest tests and includes treatment-like wording (for example, analgesic administration in a fall/fracture text pattern). These suggestions are not approved care instructions.
- The report parser is keyword-based and can misread arbitrary pasted text, units, context, negation, or copied sample text. It is not OCR or laboratory interpretation.
- The old “AI confidence” value reflected presence of input fields rather than calibrated model performance. It has been removed.
- The old image path accepted only descriptive metadata/presets yet generated “visual assessment” claims and could affect urgency. It has been changed so metadata cannot generate visual findings or urgency changes. No image model or upload is implemented.
- `clinical emergency override` language is generated by heuristics, not an approved protocol or clinician. It is not a substitute for contacting emergency services.
- The patient dashboard and historical analytics contain illustrative sample data. Clinical API routes now use owner/tenant-scoped Supabase records in the local implementation, but the schema and RLS are unverified and must not be used for real patient data.
- No clinical approval, prospective/retrospective validation, intended-use definition, hazard analysis, human factors validation, or ongoing monitoring evidence is available.
- Assessment data and patient consent are not managed as a real clinical record workflow. Do not process real health information.

## Safeguards now visible in the demo

- Intake distinguishes private patient submissions from explicitly assigned staff cases. It warns that the heuristic assessment is a demonstration and forbids real personal/health information; database ownership/isolation remains unverified.
- Result card says the rules are not clinically validated and not a diagnosis; it advises not to use the result to decide whether it is safe to wait and to seek emergency help for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.
- Image presets are labeled illustrative; the UI states that no image is uploaded or analyzed.
- Pasted report text is labeled demo-only with no upload/OCR and an unvalidated keyword-rule warning.
- Patient dashboard sample data is explicitly labeled.

## Required professional review before care use

1. Qualified clinical owners must define intended use, population, care setting, workflow, accountable human decision-makers, escalation paths, and exclusions.
2. Clinical governance must review and approve every threshold, rule, urgency mapping, alert, test/action suggestion, and emergency message; test cases should include false negatives, false positives, atypical presentations, pediatric/pregnancy/older-adult considerations, language/translation errors, incomplete data, and conflicting inputs.
3. Conduct appropriate validation and human-factors evaluation with representative data under an approved protocol. Do not use the demonstration test suite as clinical evidence.
4. Obtain qualified review of whether the product or any algorithm is subject to medical-device, clinical decision-support, professional-practice, or other requirements in the applicable jurisdiction.
5. Establish post-release monitoring, versioning, change control, incident escalation, and rollback for any clinically governed decision-support function.
6. Establish lawful purpose, transparent notices, data minimization, consent/authorization, retention, access, correction, grievance, and incident processes with qualified privacy/legal professionals before processing health data.
7. Qualified Indian privacy/legal counsel must determine the applicable requirements under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act and applicable rules, as well as any relevant health-record, telemedicine, medical-device, clinical decision-support, and professional-practice requirements. This checklist is not a legal applicability opinion or compliance claim.

## Release gate

No clinical workflow may be represented as operational, and no real patient information may be submitted, until clinical, privacy, security, and legal owners approve the intended use and the data/backend safety controls are verified. No such approval is evidenced by this audit.
